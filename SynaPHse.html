About the Project

What is SynaPHse?

An ONDC-aligned open network framework that acts as an enabling layer — integrating existing systems to facilitate seamless interaction among buyers, sellers, and service providers across platforms.

The Philippine digital economy is not limited by growth — it is limited by its inability to function as a unified, interconnected system. SynaPHse addresses this structural gap through open-standard APIs, decentralized architecture, and a cluster-based pilot in Quezon City.

Inspired by India's Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) and supported by the Philippines–India MOU on ICT Cooperation — a practical, globally aligned strategy grounded in local realities.

Interoperability Layer

A standardized gateway enabling buyers to access products from multiple seller platforms without platform-specific integration.

MSME-First Design

Meets small businesses where they already operate — Facebook, walk-in, GCash — and builds a structured, trackable layer on top.

API-Driven Architecture

Secure data exchange via open APIs. Request routing, service discovery, and transaction orchestration through the central network gateway.

Evidence-Based Impact

Built-in measurement tracks transaction frequency, MSME visibility, and digital adoption — every outcome is verifiable and real.

Implementation Process

Four Phases. 12 Months.

A structured, ground-up rollout — from validating the right communities to scaling a proven model across Metro Manila.

01
Months 0–2
Target Validation & Ground Preparation

Rapid Site Validation — Survey Cubao markets, Commonwealth food sellers, and Novaliches retail zones to confirm MSME density and adoption willingness.

Institutional Alignment — Partner with DTI and the Quezon City Government for MSME database access and existing training infrastructure.

Legal Compliance Setup — Coordinate with DTI, SEC, NPC, and PCC. Implement privacy notices, dispute features, and open-platform competition policies.

02
Months 2–5
Controlled System Deployment

Deploy SynaPHse Platform — Launch the gateway ("Tap to Join"), buyer search interface, and MSME seller onboarding form.

MSME Onboarding — Field teams visit markets physically, register sellers on-site, and train on listing products and receiving orders.

Soft Integration — Collect seller payment details (GCash/Maya), generate order records, coordinate delivery via Grab/Lalamove/LBC externally.

03
Months 5–10
Live Pilot Operation

Real Transactions Begin — Buyers discover sellers across all three QC clusters through a single platform; tracked order records generated for every transaction.

Payment & Delivery Flow — GCash/Maya payments processed externally; sellers verify receipt and update order status within SynaPHse. Delivery via Grab/LBC.

Measurement System — Weekly transaction summaries and monthly MSME performance reports track income, visibility, and digital adoption in real time.

04
Months 10–12
Evaluation & Scale Decision

Impact Assessment — Evaluate MSME income growth, transaction volume increase, and digital adoption levels based on actual platform data and user feedback.

Scale Decision — If benchmarks are met, replicate in Manila, Pasig, and Makati — sharing MSME density and digital payment infrastructure with QC clusters.

Long-Term Vision — Transition toward API-based automation and nationwide interoperability with additional funding and institutional support.
